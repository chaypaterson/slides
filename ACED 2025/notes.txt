downstream analysis

what i want to analyse: age structure and combinatorial risk factors.

biallelic loss of different tumour suppressors -- studied using age structured models. age structure gives us some estimate of the dynamics of mutations - rates and fitnesses (exp. growth rates in %/yr) - as well as the rate-limiting steps involved (see Rb)

BUT classical age structured
models can't distinguish between different variants, or different orders of
events. different combinations of these can result in loss, but may also
represent different degrees of pathogenicity (risk).

we can represent different orders of events with directed graphs. the
rates/probabilities of doing these are labels on the edges.

example: drinking a coffee and getting on a train can be done in either orders.

i would also like to structure on specific alterations. separate out p53 (to
start), and look at different types of sequence modification -- i know it will
be possible to distinguish with copy
number changes, and smaller alterations. if we can call different mutational
signatures and battenberg, we should be able to represent the different
combinations of events with these graphical models.

(i am also interested in clock-like signatures recently so i would definitely
like to see SBS1 called.)

then we can also look at different genes, and we can look at combinatorial structure and different orders of events. maybe it is worse to lose KRAS before p53? opportunity for early detection.
